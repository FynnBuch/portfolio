Designing a Robotic Arm Drive

Team project · "Konstruktionslehre 3", TU Berlin

Overview

In "Konstruktionslehre 3", the final design course of my Bachelor's at TU Berlin, our team developed the drive for a robotic arm joint. Conventional robot arms place a motor and gearbox in every joint. We took a different approach: the motor sits one joint lower, and its power is routed through a planetary gearbox and a belt to the upper joint. A second planetary gearbox there steps the torque up to what the joint requires.

It was a demanding project that pushed us to work as a team, and one that taught me a great deal.

My contribution

I was responsible for the calculation of the lower gearbox and the hydraulic brake, and for the CAD modelling of all parts in Solid Edge.

Tools

  • Solid Edge – CAD modelling
  • KISSsoft / KISSsys – gearbox calculation and simulation
  • Python – supporting calculations

Key takeaways

Beyond the technical content, the project taught me how to structure and coordinate a team effort: setting up a complete product plan, defining design steps, running design iterations and making decisions based on facts. On the technical side, I gained hands-on experience in calculating and modelling planetary gear sets and brakes.
